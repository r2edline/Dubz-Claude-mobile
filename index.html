How the rate is calculated

Profit before tax = take-home pay ÷ (1 − tax rate). Revenue needed = profit before tax + business expenses, plus your safety margin. Billable hours = (52 − weeks off) × hours per week × billable share. Your hourly rate is revenue needed ÷ billable hours.

Common questions

How do I calculate my freelance hourly rate?

Add the take-home pay you want to the tax you will owe on it and your yearly business expenses. Divide that total by the hours you can actually bill in a year, after time off and unpaid work such as admin and finding clients.

Why is a freelance rate higher than an employee's hourly wage?

A freelancer pays for their own taxes, equipment, software, insurance, holidays and sick days, and cannot bill every working hour. A rate that only matches a salary divided by 2,080 hours leaves you earning far less than that salary.

How many hours a year can a freelancer bill?

Many full-time freelancers bill between 1,000 and 1,400 hours a year. A common assumption is that 60 to 70 percent of working time is billable; the rest goes to admin, sales and learning.

Should I quote hourly or per project?

Use the hourly figure as your floor. For a project quote, estimate the hours, multiply by this rate, and add room for revisions and communication. Clients often prefer a fixed price, and you keep the gain when you work faster.
